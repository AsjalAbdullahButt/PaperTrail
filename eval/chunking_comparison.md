# Chunking Ablation: Character vs. Sentence-Boundary

This compares PaperTrail's two production chunking strategies —
`ingestion.chunk_blocks` ("character") and `ingestion.chunk_blocks_semantic`
("sentence") — run with their real production defaults (unmodified) against
the 10-document PaperTrail-documentation corpus and the 20-question test set
in `testset.json`. Numbers below come directly from `results/summary.md`,
generated by `run_eval.py`; re-running the harness regenerates both.

## Results

| Strategy | Chunks produced | Doc-level hit rate | Section-level hit rate | Avg faithfulness | Avg relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| character | 66 | 19/20 (95%) | 13/20 (65%) | n/a (offline — see Limitations) | n/a (offline) |
| sentence | 19 | 18/20 (90%) | 5/20 (25%) | n/a (offline) | n/a (offline) |

"Doc-level hit" means the correct source document appeared among the top-5
retrieved chunks. "Section-level hit" is stricter: it also requires a
retrieved chunk's attributed `section_heading` to match the test question's
expected section exactly.

## Why sentence chunking produces 3.5x fewer chunks

`chunk_blocks_semantic`'s token budget is `chunk_size * CHARS_PER_TOKEN` — at
the production default of 800 tokens and 4 characters/token, that's ~3,200
characters per chunk, about **four times** the character strategy's 800-character
window. Across our ~10 documents (each roughly 1,200-2,000 words / 7,000-12,000
characters), this means character chunking slices each document into
roughly 11-18 overlapping windows, while sentence chunking often fits an
entire document's section into one or two chunks. This is a real, unmodified
production default, not an artifact of our harness — see
`02-ingestion-and-chunking.md`.

## Concrete failure examples

**1. Doc-level miss (q05).** *"What does PaperTrail fall back to for
approximate nearest neighbor search if usearch is unavailable?"* (expected:
`03-hybrid-retrieval.md`, "Approximate nearest neighbor indexing").
- `character` retrieved a chunk from `03-hybrid-retrieval` among its top 5 — hit.
- `sentence` retrieved chunks from `04-llm-provider-and-generation-modes`,
  `01-architecture-overview`, `09-observability-and-backups`,
  `06-authentication-and-sessions`, and `07-deployment-topology` — a miss.
  Because `03-hybrid-retrieval.md`'s content is compressed into only 2
  sentence-based chunks, each one's embedding is an average over a much wider
  span of that document's vocabulary (dense retrieval, BM25, and importance
  scoring together in one doc, plus the ANN fallback detail buried mid-chunk).
  That dilutes the specific "usearch/cKDTree" signal enough that several
  unrelated documents' smaller, more topically concentrated chunks outranked it.

**2. Section-level misses despite a correct document (q01, q04, q06, and 5
more).** *"What is the default character-based chunk size and overlap?"*
(expected section: "Character-based chunking" in `02-ingestion-and-chunking.md`).
- `character` retrieved chunk `02-ingestion-and-chunking::character::5`,
  correctly attributed to the "Character-based chunking" section.
- `sentence` retrieved `02-ingestion-and-chunking::sentence::1`, which *is*
  the correct document, but that single large chunk spans from partway
  through "Two chunking strategies" through most of "Sentence-boundary
  (semantic) chunking" — its attributed `section_heading` is whatever heading
  the chunk *starts* under, which is not "Character-based chunking" even
  though that fact appears somewhere inside the chunk's text. The same
  pattern repeats for q04 and q06 against `03-hybrid-retrieval.md`: the
  correct document is retrieved, but the section label attached to the hit
  chunk is coarser than the question needs, because one chunk now covers
  several of the original document's headings.

**3. A case where coarser chunking would help, not hurt.** None of our 20
questions require synthesizing facts that character chunking split across a
chunk boundary — but this is a property of writing short, hand-crafted
questions against short documents, not evidence that mid-sentence splits are
harmless in general. `02-ingestion-and-chunking.md`'s own text documents the
theoretical case sentence chunking is meant to fix: a character-window
boundary can land inside a sentence or word, which can hand the generation
model a fragment split away from the words that give it meaning. Our corpus
is simply too short, and our questions too coarse-grained, to reliably
trigger that failure mode — a fair test of it would need longer documents
with facts stated in single long sentences that straddle an 800-character
boundary.

## Conclusion

On this corpus and test set, **character chunking retrieved the correct
document about as reliably as sentence chunking (95% vs. 90%) but was far
more precise about *which section* of that document answered the question
(65% vs. 25%)**. That gap is not really a "sentence boundaries hurt
retrieval" result — it's a side effect of the two strategies using very
different default chunk sizes (800 characters vs. ~3,200 characters), which
is how PaperTrail actually ships. The v2 roadmap's Phase 1 rationale for
sentence-boundary chunking was to stop cutting sentences mid-word so answers
read more coherently and generation has cleaner input, not to improve
retrieval hit rate — and nothing here contradicts that rationale. What this
ablation does surface is a **confound worth fixing before drawing a stronger
conclusion**: to isolate the effect of *sentence-boundary alignment* from the
effect of *chunk size*, a follow-up run should hold the character budget
constant (e.g. also cap `chunk_blocks_semantic` at ~800 characters) and
compare boundary alignment alone. As shipped, the practical takeaway is that
semantic chunking's larger chunks trade section-level citation precision for
sentence-level readability — both real, both worth having, but not free.
